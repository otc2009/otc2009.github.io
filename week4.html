Ways of Seeing

By John Berger

Episode 1

"Seeing comes before words. The child looks and recognizes before it can speak."
It's interesting to realize how much our culture and technology can influence our perceptions, especially within art. The development of the camera not only began the recording of art, but rather redefined it, letting the meaning of artistic works shift with mass reproducton. An element of genuinitiy has been lost.

Episode 2

"Men act and women appear. Men look at women. Women watch themselves being looked at."
The male gaze has shaped culture and how we percieve gender dynamics, especially within art. It's important to notice the dramatic difference in how men have been portrayed in our culture compared to how women have. It is our goal to fight these patterns with the art we produce today.

Episode 3

"Oil painting did to appearances what capital did to social relations. It reduced everything to the equality of objects. Everything became exchangeable because everything became a commodity." Paintings used to serve as statements of wealth, possession, and power. They enforced power structures by showing who had ownership over what, making them easy displays of possession. We can look at these histories to examine behaviors of how art and its ownership have shaped overtime.

Episode 4

"All publicity works upon anxiety" Publicity doesn't just show things, but it rather displays to people what they should be anxious about. These anxieties can rise in forms of comparison to others or insignificance. By tying to sensitive human emotion, PR can hurt how audiences feel about themselves and their own lives. Advertising art has taken over the social role formerly held by oil painting. We are no longer defined by what we are, but rather what we might by. Capitalism is a cancer.
